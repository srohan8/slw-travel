Rome2Rio is one of the most useful transport planning tools on the internet. This is not a piece about why it's wrong.

It's about what it's built for — and why that matters if you're trying to get somewhere without flying.

## What rome2rio optimises for

Rome2Rio's default sort order is fastest route. It surfaces the flight first because, for most people, the flight is the fastest option. It covers an enormous number of routes globally and provides price estimates alongside journey times, which makes it genuinely useful for quick comparisons.

For a traveller who is open to any mode of transport and prioritises time, it's excellent. For a traveller who has decided not to fly, it's less useful — you can filter it, but you're working against its defaults.

## What bysloth optimises for

bysloth.com was built with a single constraint: no flights. Every route it produces is surface only — trains, buses, shared taxis, ferries.

That constraint changes what gets surfaced. The train from Istanbul to Tehran runs three times a week and takes around 30 hours. It doesn't appear prominently on rome2rio. On bysloth, it's the primary connection for that corridor.

The planner also includes modes that don't appear in aggregators at all — shared taxis across Central Asian borders, the Caspian ferry between Baku and Aktau (no published timetable; the crossing takes 12–20 hours depending on conditions), marshrutka routes through the Caucasus. These are documented from sources like [Caravanistan](https://caravanistan.com) and [Seat61](https://seat61.com) rather than operator APIs, which means the data is less real-time but more likely to reflect what actually exists on the ground.

## Where rome2rio is better

For anything within Europe, rome2rio's rail and coach data is more comprehensive and more current than bysloth's. Its price estimates for European trains are generally reliable. If you're planning a trip from London to Prague or Barcelona to Lisbon, rome2rio is probably faster to use.

For short-haul domestic routes in countries with well-documented rail networks — Japan, Switzerland, the UK — there's no contest.

## Where bysloth is different

For multi-country overland routes across Central Asia, the Caucasus, the Balkans, or Southeast Asia, bysloth is built around exactly that kind of journey. It understands that the Baku–Aktau ferry has no timetable and says so. It knows the Samjhauta Express hasn't operated since 2019 and won't pretend otherwise.

It also surfaces honest uncertainty. A route from Istanbul to Kathmandu involves roughly 15–20 legs across 7–8 countries, some of which have thin documentation and one of which (the Central Asia to South Asia gap) has no viable surface connection at all. bysloth will tell you that. Rome2Rio will suggest you fly.

## The practical question

If you're trying to get from one European city to another and you're open to any mode: use rome2rio.

If you've decided not to fly and you want a planner that works with that constraint rather than against it: bysloth is built for that.

They're not competing for the same user.
